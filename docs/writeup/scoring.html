How T1, T2 and T3 are scored

The benchmark asks each model three things about every chess position, and each gets its own score. This page gives the exact scoring rules from src/evaluator.py and src/metrics.py, the corrected numbers after the October 2026 audit (Issues 14–25), and what is still uncertain. Each section has a calculator so you can try the rules yourself.

T1

Evaluation

The model gives a number in centipawns (cp), always from White's point of view. 100 cp is roughly one pawn; positive means White is better.

Prompts
cot, fen_only, pgn+fen and the isolated eval_only
Truth
One forward pass of the Lc0 network (no search), converted to centipawns and capped at ±10,000. The field is still called t1_stockfish_eval for history; no Stockfish was used (P-1).
Pre-step
The model's number is clamped to ±2000 cp. The truth is not.

Headline: rank correlation t1_spearman_rho

Spearman ρ between the model's numbers and the engine's, per model. It needs no threshold and ignores scale, so it asks only: does the model put positions in the right order? Computed as the Pearson correlation of average ranks (tie-aware).

ρ = corr( rank(model evals), rank(engine evals) ) 1 = same order · 0 = no link · −1 = reversed

Error against always answering 0 t1_mae_ratio

Most positions are near level, so a model that always answers 0 gets a small error without reading the board. Each model's mean absolute error (mate positions excluded) is divided by the error of always answering 0. Below 1 beats the lazy answer.

ratio = mean|clamp(model, ±2000) − truth| / mean|0 − truth|

Robustness only: direction and Cohen's κ t1_kappa_t{0,50,100,200}

Both numbers are labelled White, Black or Equal at threshold T. Raw agreement rises with T because more of both land on "Equal", so it is reported with κ, which subtracts the agreement expected by chance.

label(x) = "White" if x > T, "Black" if x < −T, else "Equal" κ = (agreement − chance agreement) / (1 − chance agreement)
Try it · T1, one position

ρ needs many positions, so it can't be shown for one. Try a model value of 250 with a truth of 80: the model picks the right side, yet answering 0 would have been closer.

0.11Spearman ρ, pooled
0.25best model (llama3.3:70b)
3.04×error vs always 0, pooled (1.31–7.03 per model)
0.02–0.07direction κ at every threshold

Raw direction agreement rises with the threshold; κ doesn't

Always giving the most common answer would score 48%, 54%, 79% and 93% at these thresholds, so the models are below that lazy answer at every one. The earlier write-up compared them with "33% for random guessing", which ignores class balance (Issue 14). κ shows they barely beat chance.

Still worth knowing

  • No perspective bug. ρ has the same sign with White and Black to move for every model, so no model reports from the side-to-move's view (T1-3). Llama 3.3 70B's poor direction score is noise.
  • The truth is a no-search evaluation. It is a strong network's judgement, not a deep search, which is fine for ranking positions but not exact.
T2

Move selection

The model gives a move in SAN, such as Nf6. python-chess checks it is legal. Legal moves are scored by how much worse they are than Lc0's best move.

Prompts
cot, fen_only, pgn+fen and move_only. move_only lists every legal move.
Truth
Lc0 at 800 nodes, by scripts/enrich_cpl.py. Both the "before" and "after" evals come from the same 800-node search (T2-6, Issue 22).
Rescue
An illegal answer is replaced by a legal move found elsewhere in the reply, or by re-asking with the legal-move list. Every move is labelled with where it came from (t2_move_source).

Legality, first answer and final t2_first_attempt_legal, t2_legal_attempted

Final legality counts the move after any rescue. First-answer legality counts only the model's own Move: answer, and needs the raw replies, which only the five re-run models saved.

Clamped centipawn loss t2_cpl_clamped

How much the evaluation dropped for the side to move. 0 if the move is the engine's best move. Both ends are clamped to ±1000 (Lichess convention), so a blunder can cost at most about 2000.

before = clamp(eval_before, ±1000) after = clamp(eval_after_model_move, ±1000) CPL = max(0, before − after) if White to move = max(0, after − before) if Black to move

Headline: blunder rate blunder_rates.csv

The share of legal moves with clamped CPL ≥ 1000, per model, with a seeded 95% bootstrap confidence interval. Clamped CPL piles up at its cap, so its mean is roughly "1000 × blunder rate" and hides the shape; the rate and the five buckets show it directly.

Best move against chance

How often the model plays Lc0's top move, compared with a random legal move on the same positions. Chance is the mean of 1 ÷ (number of legal moves) over the real positions (T2-3), not 1 ÷ the average number of moves.

Try it · T2

Try an eval after of −9990 (getting mated). Unclamped, the CPL would be about 10,000; clamped, it is 1150, which counts as one blunder.

Where legal moves land (all models)

Two peaks: about 19% essentially perfect and 42% blunders, with little in between.

Still worth knowing

  • Model ranks are mostly ties. Each model's interval overlaps its neighbours'. Only large gaps are real, such as Gemma 4 31B/26B (36–37%) against the rest (40–45%).
  • Legality mostly measures copying from the list. The illegal first answers are usually stock opening moves ("e4", "Nf6", "d5") that ignore the position, often for the wrong side (Issue 20).
  • Engine strength (T2-5). CPL is measured against Lc0 at 800 nodes. Re-scoring 1,000 positions (85,102 moves) at 8,000 nodes kept the ranking: Spearman ρ = 0.97 against a pre-set bar of 0.9 (a fresh 800-node re-run gives 0.99). No model moved more than 4 places, and the blunder rate went from 42.0% to 42.1%.
T3

Explanation

The model says which side is better and explains why. Scoring is rule-based, with no judge model: two points, each 0 or 1, for a total of 0–2.

Prompts
cot, fen_only, pgn+fen and the isolated explanation_only
Truth
The engine eval (P1) and the puzzle's Lichess theme (P2)
Version
t3_score_v2: whole-word theme matching (T3-1)

P1 · Side correct t3_p1_side_correct

The side the model names must match the engine's at a fixed ±50 cp threshold. Reported against the majority baseline (always naming the usually-better side) and with Cohen's κ (T3-5).

P1 = 1 if side_claimed == label(truth, T=50) else 0

P2 · Theme named t3_p2_theme_correct_v2

The theme or a synonym must appear as a whole word or phrase, allowing plural and verb endings. "estimate" no longer counts as "mate", nor "opinion" as "pin".

P2 = 1 if any \b phrase (s|es|d|ed|ing)? \b in explanation score = P1 + P2
Try it · T3

With the theme set to mate, "estimate" no longer scores. Switch the theme to backRankMate and "back rank" does.

κ 0.07side agreement beyond chance
6.0%names a tactical theme
0.31–0.76T3 score range (codellama:34b – gemma4:26b)

Still worth knowing

  • The theme mark is not used as evidence (Issue 21). The benchmark showed each puzzle's position one move before the puzzle starts, so the tactic usually isn't on the board yet. The theme mark and the difficulty tiers are reported but no conclusion rests on them. The correct positions are saved in data/puzzle_meta.json for a re-run.
  • Models rarely say "Equal". They lose P1 on level positions almost automatically, which is part of why they trail the fixed guess.
  • Words, not reasoning. The marks check that the right words appear, not that the explanation is sound.

Fine-tuning: does teaching the board change the scores?

Five models (llama3.2:3b, gemma3:4b, qwen2.5:7b, mistral:7b, deepseek-r1:7b) were trained with LoRA on 20,000 positions not in the benchmark, then compared with a re-run of the base models on the same code, positions and settings, pair by pair.

89–99%pieces found after the pieces-only fine-tune (from 1–19%)
−3 to −41 ptschange in final legality, pieces-only
65–81%legal moves found after the legal-moves fine-tune (bar was 90%)
  • Pieces only: board reading improved enormously; ρ, blunder rate and T3 did not, and final legality fell for all five.
  • Pieces + legal moves: success criteria were committed before training. Criterion 0 (legal-move recall and precision ≥ 0.9) failed for every model, so by the rule set in advance it says nothing about whether rule knowledge transfers. First-answer legality rose by at most 9.3 points (bar: 10), final legality fell for all five, and only llama3.2:3b's blunder rate fell with non-overlapping intervals.
