PR09426 · Do LLMs Reason? · Experiment 6

Is chess notation the barrier?

Every benchmark prompt described the position in FEN, a compact code that's hard even for many players. If the models failed only because they couldn't read FEN, the main conclusion would be unfair to them. This experiment removes the notation and asks again.

Final21 models · 31,487 records · 13 missing · 1 Nov 2026

01Hypothesis

H₀ Notation is not the main barrier

Given the same positions in plain English, with no FEN anywhere, the models still perform close to chance.

H₁ Notation is a major barrier

In plain English, the models' performance improves substantially.

The criteria were fixed before the experiment ran (specs/plain-board-prompts.md), so the results couldn't shape how they were judged. Each compares the plain-board prompt with the FEN prompt on the same positions.

#MeasureH₀ holds if the plain board…
N1Blunder rate (moves losing ≥ 1,000 centipawns)is less than 5 points lower than with FEN
N2Evaluation accuracy (Spearman ρ with the engine)stays below 0.20
N3Naming the better side (Cohen's κ)stays below 0.20
N4First answer is a legal movestays below 25%

Decision rule. All four hold → notation is not the main barrier. N1 drops by 5 points or more with non-overlapping confidence intervals, or ρ reaches 0.30 → notation was a major barrier. Anything else → mixed, and each measure is reported on its own.

02Experiment

  • Positions: 500 benchmark positions, 125 from each difficulty tier, drawn with a fixed seed (42).
  • Models: the 21 base models. Llama-3.3-70B is left out because it is too large to load on the test machine.
  • Questions: the same three as the main benchmark: evaluate the position, choose a move, explain who stands better.
  • Scoring: moves are scored by the Lc0 engine at 800 nodes. Results are paired by model and position, with 95% confidence intervals from bootstrapping over positions (1,000 resamples, seed 42).
  • Control: the FEN prompt runs again in the same run, so any difference comes from the prompt alone, not from a change of software or settings.

04Conclusion

Registered verdict: mixed. Removing FEN helps first-answer legality, but the pooled blunder change is small and evaluation and side agreement remain weak. These outcomes measure chess competence; they do not identify an internal reasoning mechanism.

With a legal-move list, first-answer legality rises from 25.5100% to 87.2641% on 10,490 matched requests, while paired scored blunders rise from 41.7955% to 42.5137% on a separate 10,025-pair cohort. Assistance in forming legal moves must be distinguished from move quality.

Limitations

  • One plain-text format was tested. Others, such as an image of the board, might do better.
  • Models ran at their default temperature, so single answers vary. The same-run control limits the effect on the comparison.
  • 500 positions rather than 4,000, to keep the run to about a day.
